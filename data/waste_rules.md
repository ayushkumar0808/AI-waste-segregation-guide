# Solid Waste Management Rules 2016 (India) - Plain-Language Reference Guide
# Prepared for EcoSort - AI Waste Segregation Guide (SDG 12: Responsible Consumption & Production)

## Section 1: Wet Waste (Biodegradable / Compostable)
- **Definition**: Organic, moist, and decomposable matter originating from kitchens, food preparation, dining, and gardening activities.
- **Color of Bin**: Green Bin.
- **Typical Items**: Vegetable skins and fruit peelings, leftover cooked food, tea leaves and coffee grounds, eggshells, fish and meat bones, dried leaves, garden clippings, decayed flowers.
- **Rule Clause**: Under SWM Rules 2016, Rule 4(1)(a), every waste generator shall segregate and store biodegradable waste separately and dispose of it in designated green containers for on-site composting or bio-methanation.
- **Best Practices & Reuse**: Never mix wet waste with plastic bags or foil. Compost at home or in hostel facilities to create natural fertilizer. Drain excess liquid before binning.

## Section 2: Dry Waste (Recyclable Non-Biodegradable)
- **Definition**: Clean, non-decomposable, dry waste items that can be physically sorted and sent to registered material recovery facilities (MRFs) or recycling units.
- **Color of Bin**: Blue Bin.
- **Typical Items**: Clean paper, cardboard boxes, clean plastic containers, water bottles (PET), aluminum foil (clean), tin cans, milk packets (rinsed and dried), newspapers, notebooks, glass bottles, metal caps.
- **Special Case - Soiled Paper & Pizza Boxes**: Cardboard contaminated with grease, cheese, or food oils (e.g. greasy pizza box lids) CANNOT be recycled by paper mills. Food-stained portions must be placed in Dry Waste for incineration/refuse-derived fuel (RDF) or wet waste if certified compostable, while clean lids can be recycled in Blue Bin.
- **Rule Clause**: SWM Rules 2016, Rule 4(1)(a) requires dry non-biodegradable waste to be collected in blue bins for resource recovery.
- **Best Practices & Reuse**: Rinse and dry food packaging before discarding. Flatten cardboard boxes to save bin space.

## Section 3: Domestic Hazardous Waste
- **Definition**: Waste generated at the household level containing toxic, corrosive, flammable, or bio-accumulative substances that pose risks to human health and sanitation workers.
- **Color of Bin**: Red Bin or Black/Marked Hazardous Box.
- **Typical Items**: Expired medicines, discarded paint cans, aerosol pesticide sprays, insect repellents, mercury thermometers, chemical cleaning bottles, broken fluorescent tube lights and CFL bulbs, used motor oil.
- **Rule Clause**: Under SWM Rules 2016, Rule 4(1)(c), generators must hand over domestic hazardous waste separately to authorized collectors or designated collection centers.
- **Safety Precaution**: Keep out of reach of children. Do not pour down drains or toilets. Wrap broken bulbs in old newspapers before disposal. Contact municipal authorities or hospital collection drives for bulk medical waste.

## Section 4: E-Waste (Electronic & Electrical Waste)
- **Definition**: Discarded electronic equipment, batteries, and electrical peripherals regulated under E-Waste (Management) Rules 2016/2022.
- **Color of Bin**: Yellow / Orange Bin or Designated E-Waste Drop-box.
- **Typical Items**: Old mobile phone batteries, alkaline/lithium cells, charging cords, broken earphones, printed circuit boards (PCBs), laptop chargers, dead remote controls, discarded smart devices.
- **Rule Clause**: Regulated under E-Waste (Management) Rules. Must not be mixed with municipal solid waste. Should be dropped off at authorized collection points, campus e-waste drives, or handed back to manufacturers via Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) channels.
- **Best Practices & Reuse**: Tape battery terminals to prevent short circuits. Donate functional cords or electronics to college clubs.

## Section 5: Sanitary and Biomedical Waste
- **Definition**: Personal hygiene products and small medical items generated in households or hostels.
- **Color of Bin**: Red/Hazardous wrapper or marked Sanitary pouch.
- **Typical Items**: Used sanitary napkins, baby/adult diapers, cotton swabs with body fluids, band-aids, used syringes and lancets.
- **Rule Clause**: SWM Rules 2016, Rule 4(2) states that used sanitary waste must be wrapped securely in newspaper or biodegradable pouches provided by manufacturers before disposal.
- **Safety Precaution**: Never flush sanitary products down the toilet. Wrap securely to protect sanitation workers from infections.
